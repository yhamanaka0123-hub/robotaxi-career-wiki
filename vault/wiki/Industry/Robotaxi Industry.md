# Robotaxi Industry

A robotaxi is an autonomous car (SAE level 4 or 5) operated by a ridesharing company. These services are predicted to significantly impact urban mobility, potentially reducing congestion and pollution, although challenges related to safety, public trust, and liability remain. The industry is seeing expansion in testing and development by various companies.

## Key facts

- A robotaxi is defined as an autonomous car (SAE automation level 4 or 5) operated for a ridesharing company. — [[raw/Robotaxi - Wikipedia|Robotaxi - Wikipedia]] § Introduction
- Some robotaxis caused congestion when they blocked roads due to lost cellular connectivity in 2023. — [[raw/Robotaxi - Wikipedia|Robotaxi - Wikipedia]] § Introduction
- One fatality associated with a robotaxi occurred in 2018 when a pedestrian was hit by an Uber test vehicle. — [[raw/Robotaxi - Wikipedia|Robotaxi - Wikipedia]] § Introduction
- As of 2025, the number and scope of robotaxi services is rapidly expanding, but they still all operate at a financial loss. — [[raw/Robotaxi - Wikipedia|Robotaxi - Wikipedia]] § Introduction
- An American Automobile Association survey in 2025 said that only 13% of respondents would trust a self-driving vehicle. — [[raw/Robotaxi - Wikipedia|Robotaxi - Wikipedia]] § Introduction
- Companies with active robotaxi services include Avride, Baidu, Motional, Pony.ai, Tesla, Waymo, WeRide and Zoox. — [[raw/Robotaxi - Wikipedia|Robotaxi - Wikipedia]] § Status > Services
- The cost of early vehicles was estimated in 2020 at up to US$400,000 due to custom manufacture and specialized sensors. — [[raw/Robotaxi - Wikipedia|Robotaxi - Wikipedia]] § Status > Vehicle costs
- In January 2021, Waymo stated its costs were approximately $180,000 per vehicle, and its operating cost at $0.30 per mile (~$0.19 per km). — [[raw/Robotaxi - Wikipedia|Robotaxi - Wikipedia]] § Status > Vehicle costs
